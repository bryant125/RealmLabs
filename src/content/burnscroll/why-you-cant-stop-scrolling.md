You didn't *decide* to scroll for 45 minutes. Your brain did — and it was following a design that's very good at its job.

## The loop, in plain terms

Every feed runs on **variable reward**: sometimes the next post is boring, sometimes it's amazing, and you never know which. That uncertainty is exactly what keeps a slot machine — and your thumb — moving.

- **Trigger:** boredom, a notification, a moment of stress
- **Action:** open the app
- **Reward:** an unpredictable hit of *something interesting*
- **Investment:** a like, a comment, a save — which trains the feed to pull you back

The loop isn't a willpower failure. It's a system, and you beat a system with a better system.

## The one rule: add friction, not guilt

> Willpower loses to convenience every time. So make the scroll *inconvenient* and the good habit *easy*.

Most "focus apps" just block the app and hope shame does the rest. It doesn't — you tap "ignore" and move on.

BurnScroll uses a sharper rule: **burn to unlock.** Want the app? Earn it with real movement first. The friction isn't a punishment; it's a two-second pause that hands the decision back to *you* instead of the feed.

### Try this today

1. Pick your one worst app.
2. Add a cost to opening it — steps, a short walk, anything physical.
3. Notice how often the urge fades once there's a price.

The scroll never stops because you hate it. It stops when opening it costs slightly more than it's worth. 🔥
