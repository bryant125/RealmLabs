If you searched *"newborn sleep schedule"* at 3 AM with one thumb, this article is for you. Take a breath — your baby is not broken, and neither are you.

## Why newborns don't have a "schedule" yet

In the first weeks, babies run on **wake windows**, not clock times. A wake window is simply how long your baby can comfortably stay awake before the next nap.

- **0–4 weeks:** 35–60 minutes
- **5–8 weeks:** 45–90 minutes
- **9–12 weeks:** 60–120 minutes

Watch the baby, not the clock. Yawns, red eyebrows, and a faraway stare all mean *"put me down soon."*

## A rhythm you can actually follow

> Feed → short awake time → sleep → repeat. That's it. This is the whole system.

Most calm days follow an **eat-play-sleep** loop. The order matters less than the gentleness — a rigid schedule at week three usually creates more tears (yours included).

## Track it so you don't have to remember it

The single biggest 3 AM relief is *not having to do math in your head*. Logging each feed and nap — even one-thumbed — turns "when did she last eat??" into a glance.

That's exactly why we built MamaBee: one-thumb logging and gentle insights, so the pattern reveals itself instead of you chasing it.

### The takeaway

There is no perfect schedule — only a rhythm that fits your baby this week. Track lightly, follow the wake windows, and let the pattern come to you. 🐝
